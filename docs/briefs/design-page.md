## Design rules
- The diagram must be SIMPLE: at most 16 components and about 14 chips visible at rest; one line per pair of boxes (request and reply share it); in-process calls are thin unchipped arrows; recovery or reconciliation paths are flagged cold and hidden by default. Left-to-right main path. Same viewBox as the crawler page unless you have a reason.
- A tour of 10 to 13 steps that follows ONE thing (one payment, one suspicious transaction) end to end, including the hard part (the hot account, the duplicate request, the provider outage), with token labels per leg.
- Every component has at least two real alternatives with honest for/against and one marked chosen, a failure mode, numbers, and a one-or-two sentence "say this" line.
- Notes: a clear statement of requirements (functional, non-functional with numbers), back-of-envelope arithmetic, 3 or 4 framings (how the answer changes with scale or constraints), a decisions table, failure drills (8 or more "what if X dies" with the answer), an interview script (how to spend 45 minutes; what to say first; what to draw; where to go deep), a glossary of 25 to 40 terms each tied to a component, and sources.
- Link `see` entries to this site's structure pages where they exist (ids: b-plus-tree, lsm-tree, skip-list, trie, append-log, column-store, inverted-index, geo-index, hash-table, consistent-hashing, partitioning, bloom-filter, simhash, lru-cache, cache-strategies, heap, queue, token-bucket, crawl-frontier, mvcc, two-phase-locking, two-phase-commit, leader-replication, version-vectors, crdt, quorum, raft, mapreduce, stream-joins, stream-checkpoints, tcp, realtime-push, load-balancing, serialization, stack, fifo-queue, priority-queue, linked-list, bst, avl-tree, graph).
- Plain British English, short sentences, concrete numbers, no filler, no marketing words.
