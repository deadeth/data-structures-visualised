## A. Fixes to the schema and SQL

A1. Debits the bank cannot refuse (interest, fees, settlements larger than a hold, stand-in approvals, chargeback and other reversals). The old single CHECK at -credit_limit refused them. Now two limits:
 - `credit_limit` is the soft limit. It is tested only when the customer starts a debit: in the hold's and the payment's conditional insert (`posted_balance - held_balance - $amount >= -credit_limit AND status = 'OPEN'`), under the account lock.
 - `hard_limit BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` with `CHECK (hard_limit >= credit_limit)` is the floor the database enforces: `valid_available_funds CHECK (allow_negative OR (posted_balance - held_balance) >= -hard_limit)`. Postings the bank must accept may take an account past credit_limit, as far as hard_limit. Example values: card credit_limit 500000, hard_limit 550000; a debit wallet 0 and 0 (or a small unarranged-overdraft allowance, a policy choice).
 - Past hard_limit: the customer account goes to its floor and the rest goes to that customer's own receivable account (`account_type 'RECEIVABLE'`, `owner_account_id` = the customer account, `allow_negative TRUE`, at most one per owner by a partial unique index, opened on first use). Never a pooled account. Incoming credits clear the receivable first (the Ledger API splits the credit; shown in the test as a repayment of 30000: wallet -30000, receivable +11450, card +18550).
 - Be honest about the cost: the database backstop is now the hard floor, not the exact limit. A code path that skipped the soft check could overspend by up to hard_limit - credit_limit. Say so.
 VERIFIED: at the limit a customer hold places 0 rows; statement interest 250 and a 1200 late fee post; a debit past the hard floor is refused (23514); the split to the receivable posts; a second receivable for one owner is refused; cutting credit_limit below what is used is allowed and new holds then place nothing; hard_limit below credit_limit is refused.
 The force-post SQL must also end any live hold for that authorisation in the same transaction (VERIFIED: settlement 9000 against a live hold of 8240: hold CAPTURED, wallet -9000, no receivable when the wallet can pay), and a card settlement credits the card clearing account, never an FX pool.

A2. `is_active` is replaced by `status` ('OPEN', 'NO_NEW_SPEND', 'CLOSED'). A hold needs OPEN. Entries are refused only on CLOSED. `CHECK (status <> 'CLOSED' OR (posted_balance = 0 AND held_balance = 0))`. So blocking a card stops new spend but repayments, refunds, interest and already-approved settlements still post. VERIFIED. Every place that said "set is_active FALSE" changes. Column grants now cover credit_limit, hard_limit, allow_negative, status.

A3. New `ledger_transactions` header table, never partitioned: transaction_id (primary key, made by the CALLER deterministically from the request: UUIDv5 of client id plus idempotency key, or of rail plus the scheme's own reference), kind, external_ref, description, value_date, reverses, hold_id, created_by, created_at, `UNIQUE (kind, external_ref)`. Append-only by trigger. `ledger_entries.transaction_id` references it. This is the lasting duplicate guard: VERIFIED that a replayed transaction id is refused by the header's primary key and that the same scheme reference under a new id is refused by the unique key. Remove every claim that the Ledger API "makes" a random transaction id. It also gives statements a description, corrections a `reverses` link, and postings a value date. Kept as long as the hot journal (13 months); say that older replays are refused by a rule on value_date in the API (a design choice, not tested), and its size (about 86 million rows a day at the stress load, about 100 bytes each, an estimate).

A4. The truth about the partitioned unique keys. `created_at` defaults to now(), so a replay always carries a different created_at and `UNIQUE (transaction_id, account_id, created_at)` never fires, not even within a month (a reviewer inserted the same pair twice in one month). On the partitioned journal that key guards nothing by itself. The guards are the header table's primary key (lasting) and idempotency_keys (72 hours, the saved reply). Rewrite every sentence that said "a second guard within a partition".

A5. `pending_holds` is NOT partitioned after all (this reverses the page's current choice). It stays the owner's table with `PRIMARY KEY (hold_id)`, so the hold id stays a real guard and `ON CONFLICT (hold_id) DO NOTHING` works (it errors on a table partitioned with key (hold_id, created_at): VERIFIED by two reviewers, 42P10). Growth is handled by an archive job that copies holds ended more than 90 days ago to a month-partitioned `pending_holds_history` (no uniqueness needed there) and deletes them; the hold trigger now allows DELETE only for a hold that is not ACTIVE (VERIFIED), and only the archive role has the DELETE grant. Cost: deletes leave dead rows for vacuum, about 86 million a day at the stress load. Say so, and keep partition-and-drop as the alternative with the reason it lost (the key).

A6. Lock order. The page claimed "one order everywhere"; a reviewer got a deadlock (40P01) racing a void against a capture, because void and expiry locked the hold first and the account second (through the trigger) while capture did the opposite. Rule now: every path locks the account rows first, in account_id order, then touches holds. Void: `SELECT ... FROM accounts WHERE account_id = $1 FOR NO KEY UPDATE;` then the UPDATE of the hold. Expiry job: one account per transaction: lock the account, then `UPDATE pending_holds SET status = 'EXPIRED' WHERE hold_id IN (SELECT hold_id FROM pending_holds WHERE account_id = $1 AND status = 'ACTIVE' AND expires_at < now() LIMIT 50)`, using the partial index `(account_id, expires_at) WHERE status = 'ACTIVE'`. VERIFIED on PostgreSQL 16 with real concurrent sessions: 200 rounds of capture racing void gave no errors, 128 captured and 72 voided, balances exact; the expiry job racing wallet-to-card repayments for 3 seconds expired 4,000 holds with no deadlock and no lock timeout, caches equal to journal. Remove the 1,000-row SKIP LOCKED batch.

A7. Timeouts. `SET LOCAL statement_timeout` inside the ledger function does nothing for that call: the timer is armed when the client's statement arrives (two reviewers; one cites Tom Lane on pgsql-general). `lock_timeout` set inside does work. Fix: `ALTER ROLE ledger_writer SET statement_timeout = '250ms'; ALTER ROLE ledger_writer SET lock_timeout = '50ms';` (applied at connection start, so it holds behind PgBouncer transaction pooling), plus a client deadline. VERIFIED: with the role setting, a function that sleeps 1 s is cancelled at 251 ms (57014). Batch roles (sweep, payroll, archive) get longer values. Also say: statement_timeout does not bound the COMMIT's wait for a synchronous standby; only the client deadline does.

A8. A decline is remembered. On "insufficient funds" the transaction skips the hold, saves the result on the key row and COMMITs, so a retry gets the same decline (VERIFIED). idempotency_keys is now keyed `(client_id, key)` with a `request_hash`; a reused key with a different body is refused (as Stripe does).

A9. Accounts open at zero: the guard trigger now also fires on INSERT and refuses a non-zero opening balance (VERIFIED). TRUNCATE on the journal is refused by a statement trigger (VERIFIED); DELETE and TRUNCATE grants on accounts are revoked. Soften "nobody can edit a balance" to what is true: no INSERT, UPDATE or application role can; the table owner or a superuser can disable triggers, so DDL goes through review and is logged. The break-glass `session_replication_role = replica` also switches off the append-only triggers and foreign-key checks for that transaction: say so.

A10. The zero-sum check fires once per transaction, as a deferred constraint trigger on the header row, and requires at least two entries; its query carries `created_at = NEW.created_at` so a partitioned journal is probed in one partition. The old per-entry version was quadratic (a reviewer measured 8.2 s for a 5,001-entry payroll commit). VERIFIED: unbalanced and single-entry transactions fail at COMMIT; a 5,001-entry payroll committed in about 0.7 s on the WASM build. It is ON, not optional.

A11. Statement order: page by `entry_id`, not `created_at` (created_at is the transaction's start, so two commits can land in the other order; per account, entry_id follows the account lock order). Reviewer's reasoning, not tested.

A12. One outbox row per customer account touched by a transaction (a transfer between two customers must reach both partitions). Consumers dedupe on the outbox `seq`, not on lsn.

## B. Fixes to what the page says about the world

B1. Bank, not e-money firm. State the assumption once in the brief and the opening: "We are an authorised bank. Customer balances are deposits on our balance sheet, protected by FSCS up to the limit; the ledger must produce the Single Customer View file (each depositor's eligible total) on demand. If we were an e-money institution instead, the same wallets would be safeguarded funds under CASS 15, reconciled every business day against the safeguarding accounts, and credit would be funded from the firm's own money, not customers'." Remove safeguarding language from everything that describes the bank case (account names, reconciliation, glossary, migration list). FSCS/SCV details are from reviewers' knowledge: give no figures (no limit amount, no hour count) unless already sourced on the page.

B2. The real money is in the ledger. Add asset accounts that mirror the bank's own accounts: `NOSTRO_BOE_GBP` (the settlement account behind Faster Payments and Bacs), `NOSTRO_MASTERCARD_GBP`, and nostros per currency for USD and EUR; `account_type 'NOSTRO'`, allow_negative TRUE (with credit positive, assets carry negative balances). Show the settlement posting (clearing -X, nostro +X in sign terms that balance; an incoming payment is nostro -X, wallet +X). Reconciliation compares balances at a cut-off: the nostro account against the bank statement's closing balance, and each clearing account back to zero (or aged) after its settlement cycle. Fix the tour's break example to match.

B3. Card FX. A UK issuer of a sterling card does not quote a rate or buy dollars per purchase: the network converts and sends the cardholder billing amount in GBP, and the bank's FX income is its non-sterling transaction fee. (One reviewer's reasoning plus a personal blog; mark it as not checked against scheme rules.) So example B becomes: the bakery buys USD 100.00; the authorisation arrives with a billing amount of 8000 (GBP); hold 8240 (3% buffer for rate movement before clearing, illustrative); it clears at 7900; non-sterling fee 1% = 79; entries, all GBP: wallet -7979, MASTERCARD_DEBIT_CLEARING_GBP +7900, FX_FEE_REVENUE_GBP +79; 261 freed. Keep the FX pools and the per-currency zero-sum rule, but show them where they belong: the bakery converts £8,000.00 from its GBP wallet to its USD wallet at 1 USD = 0.80 GBP: GBP wallet -800000, FX_POOL_GBP +800000; FX_POOL_USD -1000000, USD wallet +1000000 (four entries, two currencies, each sums to zero); and card spend from the USD wallet clears through MASTERCARD_DEBIT_CLEARING_USD with no conversion. Write rates as "1 USD = 0.80 GBP", never "0.80 GBP/USD". Say the owner's five-entry bridge is what a multi-currency card that settles with the network in USD would post, as the alternative.

B4. Interest. Billing accrues daily between two system accounts: INTEREST_RECEIVABLE_GBP -32, INTEREST_INCOME_GBP +32 (no customer account touched; VERIFIED it posts). On statement day it charges the card once: card -X, INTEREST_RECEIVABLE_GBP +X. If the customer pays the statement in full within the grace period, the accrual is reversed and the card never sees it. The owner's nightly debit of the card is the alternative, with why it lost (it contradicts the grace period, compounds daily, eats available credit, and failed at the limit). Example D changes: after the £649.00 capture the card is at -64900; the repayment of 30000 leaves -34900, available credit 465100; the 32p is today's accrual between system accounts.

B5. Waterfall. For regulated credit cards the FCA's CONC 6.7.4R sends repayments to the highest-interest balance first (a reviewer read Osborne Clarke's note on the 2021 amendment; cite as such). A limited company's card is mostly outside the Consumer Credit Act; sole traders and small partnerships are inside. Billing keeps balance segments by rate (purchases, cash, promotional). Replace "fees, then interest, then principal" stated as the rule.

B6. Small errors: the payroll example's pay date 2026-11-28 is a Saturday; use Friday 2026-11-27. TigerBeetle's batch limit is 8,189 per request per its docs (was 8,190). "0.80 GBP/USD" notation. `STRIPE_CLEARING_GBP`: keep the owner's name in the list but say what it would be (a clearing account for an acquirer or processor we pay out through).

B7. Failover and events. With `ANY 1 (s1, s2)`: a commit waiting for a standby holds its row locks, so if both standbys are gone every touched row queues; cancelling such a backend returns success with a warning that it may not be replicated, so the runbook never cancels them, it fences the primary. The relay could read an outbox row whose commit no standby has: set `synchronized_standby_slots` (PostgreSQL 17 and later) so logical decoding waits for the standbys, and create the Debezium slot as a failover slot so it exists on the promoted standby. The failover tool fences the old primary before promoting. Reviewer-verified in part on PG16 and from Gunnar Morling's write-up on PG17 failover slots; mark the PG17 settings as not run here. "Recovery point zero" holds for one failure at a time: say that.

## C. Gaps: give each a short stated answer (a sentence or two on the page; a push in the extras where marked *)
C1.* Cross-cluster payments in Phase 2: two local transactions joined by INTERCLUSTER_OUT on the payer's cluster and INTERCLUSTER_IN on the payee's, the second driven by the outbox and keyed by the same transaction id; the pair must net to zero and reconciliation watches the age of anything in flight. Remove "a payment never leaves its cluster".
C2.* Business date and end of day: value_date on the header; a cut-off calendar in Europe/London; an end-of-day job writes account_balances_daily (account, business date, posted, held) used by statements, interest, the Single Customer View and reconciliation; "end of day is a date, not a stop".
C3.* Card lifecycle beyond one hold and one capture: the card service keeps an authorisation record (authorised and cleared amounts, the network reference); holds are its children. Incremental authorisation = a further hold; partial clearing = capture the hold and place a new hold for the remainder in the same transaction; refund = a credit posting, no hold; chargeback = provisional credit, then the scheme's outcome posts or reverses it (a reversal the customer has spent uses the A1 path); interchange is income booked from the settlement report. State these as the answers; do not build examples for all.
C4.* Direct Debits (settle but can be reclaimed under the guarantee, so a returned debit is a posting the bank must accept: A1 path), returned Faster Payments, and that repayments restore credit at once only when the incoming payment is already final.
C5. Dual approval and limits for payments and payroll runs live in the Banking API; payroll places a hold for the run on approval with expiry past the pay date, debits on the Bacs processing day, and posts returns as their own transactions linked by `reverses`.
C6.* Load realism: 1,000 payments a second average is a deliberate stress case (86 payments per business per day; the whole UK made about 5.5 billion Faster Payments in 2025 per Pay.UK's annual report, about 15 million a day). State a reasoned baseline (10 to 20 payments per business per day is roughly 100 to 250 a second) on which Phase 1's single cluster lasts a long time, and keep the stress figures for sizing the phases.
C7. Chart of accounts: a `logical_accounts` registry (name, class, currency, GL code) and a daily summarised feed to the general ledger; FX revaluation and P&L live in the GL; currency exponents differ (JPY 0, BHD 3). One sentence each.
C8. Invariant check is incremental (accounts touched since the last watermark against the daily snapshot plus later entries); a full re-sum runs weekly on a replica.
C9. HOT and fillfactor only help while dead versions can be pruned: no long transactions on the primary, `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout`, an alert on the oldest xmin (a reviewer measured the split rows' table grow from 8 pages to 469 with one old transaction open).
C10. TigerBeetle: a transfer has one debit and one credit, so multi-leg postings become linked transfers and each currency is its own ledger; there is no credit_limit column, so limits are modelled with balance flags and funding transfers; triggers, guard and CHECK disappear, so those rules move into the Ledger API. (Reviewers' knowledge, unverified.)
